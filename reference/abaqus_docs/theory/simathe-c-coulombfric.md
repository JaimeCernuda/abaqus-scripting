Rate-dependent friction cannot be used in a static Riks analysis since velocity is not defined. In Abaqus it is possible to put a limit on the critical stress:  τc⁢r⁢i⁢t=m⁢i⁢n⁢(μ⁢p,τm⁢a⁢x),  where τm⁢a⁢x is user-specified. If the equivalent stress is at the critical stress (τe⁢q=τc⁢r⁢i⁢t), slip can occur. If the friction is isotropic, the direction of the slip and the frictional stress coincide, which is expressed in the form  τiτe⁢q=γ˙iγ˙e⁢q,  where γ˙i is the slip rate in direction i and γ˙e⁢q is the magnitude of the slip velocity,  γ˙e⁢q=γ˙12+γ˙22.  As will be shown later, the same laws can be used for anisotropic friction after some simple transformations.  The above behavior can be modeled in Abaqus/Standard in two different ways. By default, the condition of no relative motion is approximated by stiff elastic behavior. The stiffness is chosen such that the relative motion from the position of zero shear stress is bounded by a value γc⁢r⁢i⁢t. (In [Frictional Behavior](.._SIMACAEITNRefMap_simaitn-c-friction.md#simaitn-c-friction) γc⁢r⁢i⁢t is referred to as the allowable maximum elastic slip.) The critical slip value, γc⁢r⁢i⁢t, can be specified by the user. If it is not specified by the user, γc⁢r⁢i⁢t is, by default, set to 0.5% of the average length of all contact elements in the model. It is worth noting that this approximate implementation method can also be considered an implementation of a nonlocal friction model; that is, a friction model for which the Coulomb condition is not applied pointwise but weighted over a small area with a so-called mollifying function ([Zhong, 1989](simathe-c-references.md#simathe-c-references__simathe-c-ref-zhong)). See [Oden and Pires (1983)](simathe-c-references.md#simathe-c-references__simathe-c-ref-oden-1983) for further discussion of nonlocal friction models.  Optionally, the relative motion in the absence of slip can be made exactly zero with the use of a Lagrange multiplier formulation. Although this procedure appears attractive because of the exact sticking constraint, it has two disadvantages:   1. The additional Lagrange multipliers increase the cost of the analysis. 2. The presence of rigid constraints tends to slow or sometimes prevent    convergence of the Newton solution scheme used in    Abaqus/Standard.    This is likely to occur in areas where contact conditions change.   A special case of friction in Abaqus/Standard is so-called rough friction, where it is assumed that there is no bound on the shear stress; that is, no relative motion can occur as long as the surfaces are in contact. Rough friction is implemented with the Lagrange multiplier method.  In Abaqus/Explicit the relative motion in the absence of slip is always equal to zero if the kinematic contact algorithm is used with hard tangential surface behavior; at the end of each increment the positions of the nodes on the contact surfaces are adjusted so that the relative motion is zero. With the penalty contact algorithm in Abaqus/Explicit the relative motion in the absence of slip is equal to the friction force divided by the penalty stiffness.    Elastic stick formulation In the elastic stick formulation in Abaqus/Standard, the “elastic” tangential slip γie⁢l is defined as the reversible relative tangential motion from the point of zero shear stress. The elastic slip is related to the interface shear stress with the relation  τi=ks⁢γie⁢l,  where ks is the (current) “stiffness in stick,” which follows from the relation  ks=τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγc⁢r⁢i⁢t.  Since τc⁢r⁢i⁢t may be dependent on contact pressure, slip rate, average surface temperature at the contact point, and field variables, ks may change during the analysis. The behavior remains elastic as long as the equivalent stress does not exceed the critical stress; hence,  γie⁢l⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=γie⁢l⁢(t)+Δ⁢γi.  Consistent linearization of this expression yields  (1)d⁢τi=ksdγi                                   if        τc⁢r⁢i⁢t=τm⁢a⁢x,d⁢τi=ksdγi+τiτc⁢r⁢i⁢t(μ+p∂⁡μ∂⁡p)  dp     if        τc⁢r⁢i⁢t=μp.  The contributions from the contact pressure are nonsymmetric for the second case. Since the slip rate is zero in the elastic stick formulation, derivatives with respect to the slip velocity are not needed.  The above expressions hold if the equivalent shear stress remains less than the critical stress. If the equivalent stress exceeds the critical stress, slip must be taken into consideration so that the condition τe⁢q=τc⁢r⁢i⁢t is satisfied. Let the starting situation be characterized by the elastic slip γ¯ie⁢l. The critical stress at the end of the increment follows from the contact pressure, p, and the slip rate, γ˙e⁢qs⁢l.  Let the (as yet unknown) elastic slip at the end of the increment be γie⁢l and the slip increment be Δ⁢γis⁢l. Consistency requires that  Δ⁢γi=γie⁢l-γ¯ie⁢l+Δ⁢γis⁢l,  and the shear stress at the end of the increment follows from the elasticity relation,  τi=ks⁢γie⁢l=τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγc⁢r⁢i⁢t⁢γie⁢l.  The slip increment is related to the stress at the end of the increment with the backward difference approach:  Δ⁢γis⁢l=τiτc⁢r⁢i⁢t⁢Δ⁢γe⁢qs⁢l.  With these equations and the critical stress equality τe⁢q=τc⁢r⁢i⁢t, it is possible to solve for γie⁢l, Δ⁢γis⁢l, and τi. Elimination of γie⁢l and Δ⁢γis⁢l from the above equations yields,  Δ⁢γi=τiτc⁢r⁢i⁢t⁢γc⁢r⁢i⁢t-γ¯ie⁢l+τiτc⁢r⁢i⁢t⁢Δ⁢γe⁢qs⁢l  or  τi=γ¯ie⁢l+Δ⁢γiγc⁢r⁢i⁢t+Δ⁢γe⁢qs⁢l⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢t.  It is convenient to define the “elastic predictor strain”  γip⁢r⁢=def⁢γ¯ie⁢l+Δ⁢γi,  which simplifies the expression for the stress to  τi=γip⁢rγc⁢r⁢i⁢t+Δ⁢γe⁢qs⁢l⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢t.  Substitution in the critical stress equality yields  Δ⁢γe⁢qs⁢l=γe⁢qp⁢r-γc⁢r⁢i⁢t,  where  γe⁢qp⁢r⁢=def⁢(γ1p⁢r)2+(γ2p⁢r)2.  Substitution in the expression for τi and introduction of the normalized slip direction ni=γip⁢r/γe⁢qp⁢r furnishes the final result  (2)τi=ni⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢t.  Here τc⁢r⁢i⁢t is a function of the slip rate, which is obtained with  γ˙e⁢qs⁢l=Δ⁢γe⁢qs⁢l/Δ⁢t,  where Δ⁢t is the time increment in a static analysis. In the case of dynamics with the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator, Δ⁢t is scaled by the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator parameters, γ and β.  For the iterative solution scheme this equation must be linearized. Some straightforward algebra yields  d⁢τi=​d⁢γip⁢rγe⁢qp⁢r⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢t-ni⁢nj⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγe⁢qp⁢r⁢d⁢γjp⁢r+ni⁢d⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢t=(δi⁢j-ni⁢nj)⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγe⁢qp⁢r⁢d⁢γjp⁢r+ni⁢(∂⁡τc⁢r⁢i⁢t∂⁡p⁢d⁢p+∂⁡τc⁢r⁢i⁢t∂⁡γ˙e⁢qs⁢l⁢d⁢γ˙e⁢qs⁢l).  With the expression for the equivalent slip the final result is  (3)d⁢τi=​(δi⁢j-ni⁢nj)⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγe⁢qp⁢r⁢d⁢γj⁢                                                   ⁢if    τcrit=τmax;d⁢τi=​(δi⁢j-ni⁢nj)⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγe⁢qp⁢r⁢d⁢γj+ni⁢(μ+p⁢∂⁡μ∂⁡p)⁢d⁢p+ni⁢nj⁢pΔ⁢t⁢∂⁡μ∂⁡γ˙e⁢q⁢d⁢γj⁢   ⁢if    τcrit=μ⁢p.  In this case the unsymmetric terms may have a strong effect on the speed of convergence of the Newton scheme. Hence, use of the unsymmetric equations solver is strongly recommended for the analysis of problems in which sliding friction occurs. In the case of dynamics with the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator, Δ⁢t is scaled by the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator parameters.  Frictional stress due to forced sliding in static analysis If the user specifies different velocities for two bodies in contact as a predefined field, it is assumed that the slip velocity follows from the prescribed motion and is independent of the displacement values. The frictional stresses then follow from  τi=τe⁢q⁢γ˙iγ˙e⁢q,  where  τe⁢q=μ⁢p,  and  γ˙e⁢q=(γ˙1)2+(γ˙2)2.  γ˙i are the tangential slip velocities derived from the user-defined fields (i=1,2).  Friction contributions for complex eigenvalue extraction analysis At the contact nodes at which the velocity differential is imposed, the linearized shear stress can be expressed in the following form:  d⁢τi=(δi⁢j-ni⁢nj)⁢τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγ˙eqpr⁢d⁢γ˙j+ni⁢(μ+p⁢∂⁡μ∂⁡p)⁢d⁢p+ni⁢nj⁢p⁢∂⁡μ∂⁡γ˙e⁢q⁢d⁢γ˙j.  In the equation above the first term is generated by the friction forces acting in the direction perpendicular to the direction of slip. The third term exists if the friction coefficient depends on velocity. In the complex eigenvalue extraction analysis both terms contribute to the damping matrix. The second term contributes to the stiffness matrix.  Viscous stick formulation for steady-state transport For steady-state transport a viscous stick formulation is used. In this case the “viscous” tangential slip rate, γ˙iv, is related to the interface shear stress by  τi=κs⁢γ˙iv,  where κs is the “stick viscosity,” which follows from the relation  κs=τc⁢r⁢i⁢tγ˙c⁢r⁢i⁢t.  The allowable viscous slip is defined as a fraction of the relative tangential velocity  γ˙c⁢r⁢i⁢t=2⁢Ff⁢ω⁢R,  where Ff is a user-defined slip tolerance, ω is the angular spinning velocity, and R is the radius of a point on the contact surface in the undeformed configuration.  Exact stick formulation In Abaqus/Standard Lagrange multipliers are used to enforce exact sticking conditions. A constraint term enforced with Lagrange multipliers δ⁢qi is added to the virtual work statement:  (4)δ⁢Π\*=∫S(τi⁢δ⁢γi+Δ⁢γi⁢δ⁢qi)⁢d⁢S,  where δ⁢qi is a Lagrange multiplier. By taking the rate of change of [Equation 4](simathe-c-coulombfric.md#simathe-c-coulombfric-t-ExactStickFormulation-sma-topic5__simathe-c-coulombfric-eq4), the rate of virtual work is obtained:  (5)d⁢δ⁢Π\*=∫S(d⁢τi⁢δ⁢γi+d⁢γi⁢δ⁢qi+τi⁢d⁢δ⁢γi)⁢d⁢S,  where the last term results from a nonlinear relation between nodal displacements and relative motion γi. This term is nonzero only for contact with finite sliding (see [Finite-sliding interaction between deformable bodies](simathe-c-slidecontactelem.md) and [Finite-sliding interaction between a deformable and a rigid body](simathe-c-defbodyrigidsurf.md)).  To obtain a complete formulation, a relationship between Δ⁢γi, qi, and τi must be defined. For sticking conditions a suitable relation is  τi=qi+k0⁢Δ⁢γi,  with k0 a reference stiffness selected internally in Abaqus. The reference stiffness can be considered to model a spring that is in parallel to the sticking constraint. Since the constraint prevents relative motion, the reference stiffness has no physical significance and is added only to eliminate zero terms on the diagonal of the stiffness matrix that could cause equation solver problems. The variational and rate forms of the equation for τi are readily obtained as  δ⁢τi=δ⁢qi+k0⁢δ⁢γi        and        d⁢τi=d⁢qi+k0⁢d⁢γi.  Substitution in [Equation 5](simathe-c-coulombfric.md#simathe-c-coulombfric-t-ExactStickFormulation-sma-topic5__simathe-c-coulombfric-eq5) yields  (6)d⁢δ⁢Π\*=∫S(k0⁢δ⁢γi⁢d⁢γi+δ⁢γi⁢d⁢qi+δ⁢qi⁢d⁢γi+τi⁢d⁢δ⁢γi)⁢d⁢S.  If the element is slipping, Δ⁢γi can take an arbitrary value. Consequently the terms involving δ⁢qi in [Equation 4](simathe-c-coulombfric.md#simathe-c-coulombfric-t-ExactStickFormulation-sma-topic5__simathe-c-coulombfric-eq4) and [Equation 5](simathe-c-coulombfric.md#simathe-c-coulombfric-t-ExactStickFormulation-sma-topic5__simathe-c-coulombfric-eq5) vanish. With the backward difference method, the frictional stress is obtained as  (7)τi=τc⁢r⁢i⁢t⁢Δ⁢γiΔ⁢γe⁢q,  and the linearized form is  (8)d⁢τi=​τc⁢r⁢i⁢tΔ⁢γe⁢q⁢(δi⁢j-ni⁢nj)⁢d⁢γj⁢                   ⁢if    τcrit=τmax,d⁢τi=​τc⁢r⁢i⁢tΔ⁢γe⁢q⁢(δi⁢j-ni⁢nj)⁢d⁢γj+ni⁢(μ+p⁢∂⁡μ∂⁡p)⁢d⁢p+ni⁢nj⁢pΔ⁢t⁢∂⁡μ∂⁡γ˙e⁢q⁢d⁢γj⁢  ⁢if    τcrit=μ⁢p.  The rate of virtual work can, hence, be written in the form  (9)d⁢δ⁢Π\*=​∫S[τc⁢r⁢i⁢tΔ⁢γe⁢q⁢(δi⁢j-ni⁢nj)⁢δ⁢γi⁢d⁢γj+τi⁢d⁢δ⁢γi]⁢d⁢S⁢         ⁢if    τcrit=τmax,d⁢δ⁢Π\*=​∫S[τc⁢r⁢i⁢tΔ⁢γe⁢q(δi⁢j-ninj)δγidγj+(μ+p∂⁡μ∂⁡p)niδγidp+pΔ⁢t∂⁡μ∂⁡γ˙e⁢qninjδγidγj+τidδγi]dS            if    τ​crit=μp.  Observe that for two-dimensional problems the term involving (δi⁢j-ni⁢nj) vanishes. In the case of dynamics with the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator, Δ⁢t is scaled by the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator parameters.  To determine whether an element sticks or slips, the following tests are used. If the element is currently sticking, it is checked whether the magnitude of the frictional stress exceeds the critical value. Hence, if  τe⁢q>τc⁢r⁢i⁢t,  the state is changed to slipping.  If the element is slipping, the direction of slip is compared with the frictional stress τi⁢(t) applied at the start of the iteration. If at the end of the iteration  Δ⁢γi⁢τi⁢(t)<0,  the state is changed to sticking.  Anisotropic friction In anisotropic friction the friction coefficients in the two (principal) directions are different; hence, the critical shear stresses are different:  τ1c⁢r⁢i⁢t=μ1⁢p        and        τ2c⁢r⁢i⁢t=μ2⁢p.  The two critical shear stresses are at the extreme points of the friction ellipse, given by the equation  (τ1μ1)2+(τ2μ2)2=p2.  This suggests definition of the scaled shear stresses,  (10)τ1¯⁢=def⁢μ¯μ1⁢τ1        and        τ2¯⁢=def⁢μ¯μ2⁢τ2,  where μ¯ is the average friction coefficient defined by  μ¯=12⁢(μ12+μ22).  Hence, the (scaled) equivalent frictional stress is defined by  τ¯e⁢q=τ1¯2+τ¯22,  and the average critical stress  τ¯c⁢r⁢i⁢t=m⁢i⁢n⁢(μ¯⁢p,τm⁢a⁢x),  where τm⁢a⁢x is the user-specified limiting shear stress, which is applied to the scaled shear stresses. No slip will occur if τ¯e⁢q<τ¯c⁢r⁢i⁢t, and slip can occur if τ¯e⁢q=τ¯c⁢r⁢i⁢t.  If slip occurs, it is assumed that the direction of slip is governed by an associated slip law:  γ˙1=λ⁢∂⁡τ¯e⁢q∂⁡τ1=λ⁢∂⁡τ¯e⁢q∂⁡τ¯1⁢d⁢τ¯1d⁢τ1=λ⁢τ¯1τ¯e⁢q⁢μ¯μ1        and        γ˙2=λ⁢∂⁡τ¯e⁢q∂⁡τ2=λ⁢∂⁡τ¯e⁢q∂⁡τ¯2⁢d⁢τ¯2d⁢τ2=λ⁢τ¯2τ¯e⁢q⁢μ¯μ2.  Hence, scaled rates of slip are defined by  (11)γ¯˙1⁢=def⁢μ1μ¯⁢γ˙1        and        γ¯˙2⁢=def⁢μ2μ¯⁢γ˙2,  and the (scaled) equivalent slip rate  γ¯˙e⁢q=γ¯˙12+γ¯˙22.  As a result, the direction of the scaled slip and the scaled shear stress coincide:  τ¯iτ¯e⁢q=γ¯˙iγ¯˙e⁢q.  Since these equations have exactly the same structure as the equations for isotropic friction, the same solution algorithms can be used to get the incremental and linearized equations in terms of the scaled stress and slip. The actual stresses are readily obtained from the scaled stresses with [Equation 10](simathe-c-coulombfric.md#simathe-c-coulombfric-anisotropic__simathe-c-coulombfric-eq10), and the linearized equations are scaled with the same factors.  In the anisotropic elastic stick formulation, it is assumed that the stiffness in stick in terms of the scaled stress and slip is constant:  k¯s=τ¯c⁢r⁢i⁢tγc⁢r⁢i⁢t;  hence, the scaled stress is related to the scaled elastic slip by the relation  τ¯i=k¯s⁢γ¯ie⁢l.  In terms of the actual stress and strain in the 1- and 2-directions, this implies  τ1=k1⁢γ1e⁢l        and        τ2=k2⁢γ2e⁢l,  with  k1=(μ1μ¯)2⁢k¯s,                k2=(μ2μ¯)2⁢k¯s.  Hence, the anisotropy in terms of the stiffness in stick is more pronounced than the anisotropy in terms of critical stress.  Viscous damping In addition to the friction models described above, Abaqus allows for the definition of a “viscous” shear stress τiv that is proportional to the relative tangential velocity γ˙i. This viscous damping in the tangential direction occurs if   * damping in the contact direction is included and the tangent fraction is   nonzero; or * contact controls are used to stabilize rigid body modes automatically in   multi-body contact analysis. The virtual work contribution associated with the viscous shear stress is  δ⁢Π=τiv⁢δ⁢γi. In static analysis the velocity is defined as the displacement increment divided by the time increment. Therefore, γ˙j=Δ⁢γj/Δ⁢t, and the stiffness contribution reduces to  d⁢δ⁢Π=δ⁢γi⁢d⁢μvd⁢h⁢Δ⁢γiΔ⁢t⁢d⁢h+δ⁢γi⁢μvΔ⁢t⁢d⁢γi.  The previous expression also applies to dynamics with the backward Euler time integration operator. In the case of dynamics with the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator, ∂⁡γ˙j/∂⁡γk is defined by the dynamic time integration operator and the stiffness contribution can be written as  d⁢δ⁢Π=δ⁢γi⁢d⁢μd⁢h⁢γ˙i⁢d⁢h+δ⁢γi⁢γ⁢μβ⁢Δ⁢t⁢d⁢γi,  where γ and β are the Hilber-Hughes-Taylor time integration operator parameters. Viscous damping cannot be used in a Riks analysis since velocity is not defined. |
