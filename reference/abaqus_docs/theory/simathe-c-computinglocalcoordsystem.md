The transformation matrix in this case will be  [cos⁡θsin⁡θ0-sin⁡θcos⁡θ0001].  Three-dimensional case When performing three-dimensional stress linearization, the local x-axis will be defined by the stress line. The local y- and z-axes are computed by a series of cross products. This procedure is shown below.  The vector between points (X1, Y1, Z1) and (X2, Y2, Z2) is  x=[Δ⁢XΔ⁢YΔ⁢Z]=[X2-X1Y2-Y1Z2-Z1].  Assuming the local y-axis lies in the plane of the local x-axis and the global Y-axis, the local z-axis is defined by  z​=x×y=det[ijkΔ⁢XΔ⁢YΔ⁢Z010]=[-Δ⁢Z0Δ⁢X].  Therefore, the local y-axis will be  y​=z×x=det[ijk-Δ⁢Z0Δ⁢XΔ⁢XΔ⁢YΔ⁢Z]=[-Δ⁢X⁢Δ⁢YΔ⁢X2+Δ⁢Z-Δ⁢Y⁢Δ⁢Z].  After normalization, the above three vectors can be combined to create the transformation matrix. |
