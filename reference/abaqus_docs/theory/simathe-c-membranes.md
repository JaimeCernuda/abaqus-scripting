The basis system is defined by the standard convention used in Abaqus for a basis on a surface in space. In this section Greek indices take the range 1, 2, and Latin indices take the range 1, 2, 3. Greek indices are used to refer to components in the first two directions of the local orthonormal basis (in the surface of the membrane).    Equilibrium The virtual work contribution from the internal forces in a membrane element is  (1)δ⁢WI=∫Vσ:δ⁢ε⁢d⁢V,  where σ is the Cauchy stress, δ⁢ε=sym⁢(δ⁢L) is the virtual rate of deformation (δ⁢L=∂⁡δ⁢u/∂⁡x, where δ⁢u is the virtual velocity field), and V is the current volume of the membrane.  We assume that only the membrane stress components in the surface of the membrane are nonzero: σ3⁢i=0. Then [Equation 1](simathe-c-membranes.md#simathe-c-membranes-t-Equilibrium-sma-topic1__simathe-c-membranes-eq1) simplifies to  δ⁢WI​=∫Vσα⁢β⁢δ⁢εα⁢β⁢d⁢V​=∫Vσα⁢β⁢δ⁢Lα⁢β⁢d⁢V⁢ (since σ is symmetric),  where  δ⁢Lα⁢β=eα⁢∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x⋅eβ=eα⁢∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡xβ  and d⁢V=t⁢d⁢A, where t is the current thickness of the element and A is its current area.  Jacobian The consistent Jacobian contribution from the element is  dδWI=∫At(δε:D:dε+σ:(δLT⋅dL-2δε⋅dε))dA.  Since we assume that σ3⁢i=0, the first term in the integrand is  δ⁢εα⁢β⁢Dα⁢β⁢γ⁢δ⁢d⁢εγ⁢δ.  We also assume that there is no transverse shear strain of the element: ε3⁢i=0, and, hence, δ⁢εα⁢i⁢d⁢εi⁢β=δ⁢εα⁢γ⁢d⁢εγ⁢β. Thus, the second term in the integrand is  σα⁢β⁢(∂⁡u∂⁡xα⋅∂⁡u∂⁡xβ-12⁢(eγ⋅∂⁡u∂⁡xα+eα⋅∂⁡u∂⁡xγ)⁢(eγ⋅∂⁡u∂⁡xβ+eβ⋅∂⁡u∂⁡xγ)).  We can write this out as  σ11⁢∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+σ22⁢∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2+σ12⁢(∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2+∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1)-2⁢σ11⁢e1⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1⁢e1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1-2⁢σ22⁢e2⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2⁢e2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2-12⁢(σ11+σ22)⁢(e2⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1⁢e2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+e1⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2⁢e1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2+e1⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2⁢e2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+e2⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1⁢e1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2)-σ12(e1⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1e1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+e2⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1e2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2+e1⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2e1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+e2⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2e2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+e1⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2e2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2+e2⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1e1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+e1⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x1e2⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x1+e2⋅∂⁡δ⁢u∂⁡x2e1⋅∂⁡d⁢u∂⁡x2)  Thickness change In geometrically nonlinear analyses the cross-section thickness changes as a function of the membrane strain with a user-defined “effective section Poisson's ratio,” ν.  In plane stress σ33=0; linear elasticity gives  ϵ33=-ν1-ν⁢(ϵ11+ϵ22).  Treating these as logarithmic strains,  ln⁡(tt0)=-ν1-ν⁢(ln⁡(l1l10)+ln⁡(l2l20))=-ν1-ν⁢ln⁡(AA0),  where A is the area on the membrane's reference surface. This nonlinear analogy with linear elasticity leads to the thickness change relationship:  tt0=(AA0)-ν1-ν.  For ν=0.5 the material is incompressible; for ν=0 the section thickness does not change.  Total deformation The deformation gradient is F=∂⁡x/∂⁡X. Since we take e3 normal to the current membrane surface and assume no transverse shear of the membrane,  F3⁢α=e3⋅∂⁡x∂⁡Xα=0    and    Fα⁢3=eα⋅∂⁡x∂⁡X3=0.  By the thickness change assumption above, the direct out-of-plane component of the deformation gradient is  F33=1(F11⁢F22-F12⁢F21)ν1-ν.  To calculate the deformation gradient at the end of the increment, first we calculate the two tangent vectors at the end of the increment defined by the derivative of the position with respect to the reference coordinates:  ∂⁡xn+1∂⁡Xβ=∂⁡xn+1∂⁡ξα⁢∂⁡ξα∂⁡Xβ,  where ∂⁡xn+1/∂⁡ξα is obtained by interpolation with the shape function derivatives from the nodal coordinates and the change of coordinate transformation ∂⁡ξα/∂⁡Xβ is based on the reference geometry. The deformation gradient components are defined  Fα⁢β=eαn+1⋅∂⁡xn+1∂⁡Xβ.  To choose the element basis directions eαn+1, we do the following. Find any pair of in-plane orthonormal vectors e^αn+1 (by the standard Abaqus projection). Then find the angle Δ⁢ψ such that the element basis vectors eαn+1, defined  e1n+1​=cos⁡(Δ⁢ψ)⁢e^1n+1+sin⁡(Δ⁢ψ)⁢e^2n+1e2n+1​=-sin⁡(Δ⁢ψ)⁢e^1n+1+cos⁡(Δ⁢ψ)⁢e^2n+1,  satisfy the symmetry condition  Fα⁢β=eαn+1⋅∂⁡xn+1∂⁡Xβ=Fβ⁢α.  Using the definitions of eαn+1 in terms of e^αn+1 in the above equation, the rotation angle Δ⁢ψ is found to be  Δ⁢ψ=tan-1⁡[F^21-F^12F^11+F^22],  where  F^α⁢β⁢=def⁢e^αn+1⋅∂⁡xn+1∂⁡Xβ.  The deformation gradient then follows immediately.  For elastomers we work directly in terms of Bα⁢β=Fα⁢γ⁢Fβ⁢γ and B33=(F33)2. For inelastic material models we need measures of incremental strain and average material rotation, which we compute from Δ⁢F defined by F=Δ⁢F⋅Fn, where Fn is the deformation gradient at the start of the current increment (at increment “n”):  (Fn)α⁢β=(en)α⋅∂⁡xn∂⁡Xβ.  We can define the components of Δ⁢F-1 by  (Δ⁢F-1)α⁢β=(en)α⋅∂⁡xn∂⁡xβ  and, hence, define Δ⁢Fα⁢β by inversion.  The incremental strain and rotation are then defined from the polar decomposition Δ⁢F=Δ⁢V⋅Δ⁢R, where Δ⁢R is a rotation matrix and Δ⁢V is a pure stretch:  Δ⁢V=∑I=13Δ⁢λI⁢aI⁢aI  (see [Deformation](simathe-c-deformation.md)). We find the Δ⁢λI and the corresponding eigenvectors aI by solving the eigenproblem for  Δ⁢F⋅Δ⁢FT=Δ⁢V⋅Δ⁢V=∑I=13(Δ⁢λI)2⁢aI⁢aI.  Since we assume no transverse shear in the membrane, the normal direction (along e3) is always a principal direction, so the eigenproblem is the 2×2 problem  Δ⁢Fα⁢γ⁢Fβ⁢γ=∑I=12(Δ⁢λ)2⁢aαI⁢aβI.  The logarithmic strain increment is then  Δ⁢εα⁢β=∑I=12ln⁡(Δ⁢λI)⁢aαI⁢aβI,  and the average material rotation increment is defined from the polar decomposition of the increment:  Δ⁢Rα⁢β=∑I=121Δ⁢λI⁢aαI⁢aγI⁢Δ⁢Fγ⁢β.  Due to the choice of the element basis directions, we can assume that  Δ⁢Rα⁢β≈δα⁢β. |
