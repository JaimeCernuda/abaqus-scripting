Further, it is assumed that the cross-section does not deform in its plane or warp out of its plane, and that this cross-sectional plane remains normal to the beam axis. These are the classical assumptions of the Euler-Bernoulli beam theory, which provides satisfactory results for slender beams.  Let (S,S1,S2) be material coordinates such that S locates points on the beam axis and (S1,S2) measures distance in the cross-section. In addition, let n1, n2 be unit vectors normal to the beam axis in the current configuration: n1=n1⁢(S), n2=n2⁢(S). Then the position of a point of the beam in the current configuration is  xf=x+S1⁢n1+S2⁢n2,  where x=x⁢(S) is the point on the beam axis of the cross-section containing xf. Then  d⁢xfd⁢S=d⁢xd⁢S+S1⁢d⁢n1d⁢S+S2d⁢n2d⁢S,  and so length on the fiber at (S,S1,S2) is measured in the current configuration as  (d⁢lf)2​=d⁢xfd⁢S⋅d⁢xfd⁢S⁢(d⁢S)2​=(d⁢xd⁢S+S1⁢d⁢n1d⁢S+S2⁢d⁢n2d⁢S)⋅(d⁢xd⁢S+S1⁢d⁢n1d⁢S+S2d⁢n2d⁢S)⁢(d⁢S)2.  Now since the beam is slender, we will neglect terms of second-order in S1 and S2, the distance measuring material coordinates in the cross-section. Thus,  (1)(d⁢lf)2=(d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢xd⁢S+2⁢S1⁢d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n1d⁢S+2⁢S2d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n2d⁢S)⁢(d⁢S)2.    Strain measures The internal virtual work rate associated with axial stress is  δ⁢W1I=∫Lf∫Aσf⁢δ⁢εf⁢d⁢Ad⁢Lf,  where σfand δ⁢εf are any material stress and strain measures associated with axial deformation at the point (S,S1,S2) of the beam, since strains are assumed to be small. For this purpose we will use Green's strain so that  εf=12⁢[(λf)2-1],  where (λf)2=(d⁢lf)2/(d⁢Lf)2, the square of the ratio of current configuration length to reference configuration length in the axial direction on the fiber. From [Equation 1](simathe-c-eulerbernbeams.md#simathe-c-eulerbernbeams__simathe-c-eulerbernbeams-eq1) and its equivalent in the reference configuration, we have  εf⁢d⁢Lf=12[(d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢xd⁢S+2S1d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n1d⁢S+2S2d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n2d⁢S)⋅(d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢xd⁢S+2S1d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢N1d⁢S+2S2d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢N2d⁢S)-1-1]​⋅(d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢Xd⁢S+2⁢S1⁢d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢N1d⁢S+2⁢S2⁢d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢N2d⁢S)12⁢d⁢S.  Again, neglecting all but first-order terms in S1 and S2 because of the slenderness assumption, this becomes  εfdLf≈[ε+S1⁢G-1⁢(d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n1d⁢S-(1+ε)⁢d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢N1d⁢S)+S2G-1(d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n2d⁢S-(1+ε)d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢N2d⁢S)]dL,  where  G=d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢Xd⁢Sandε=12⁢(λ2-1)=12⁢[(d⁢ld⁢L)2-1]is the Green’s strain of the beam axis.  This simplification allows us to write the internal virtual work rate associated with axial stress as  δ⁢W1I=∫Lf∫Aσf⁢δ⁢εf⁢d⁢A⁢  d⁢Lf=∫L∫Aσf⁢δ⁢ε~f⁢d⁢A⁢  d⁢L,  where  ε~f=ε-S1⁢K2+S2⁢K1,  with  ε​=12⁢(λ2-1)     as Green’s strain of the beam axis,K2​=-G-1⁢(d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n1d⁢S-(1+ε)⁢d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢N1d⁢S)  and  K1=-G-1⁢(d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n2d⁢S-(1+ε)⁢d⁢Xd⁢S⋅d⁢N2d⁢S).  Now the cross-sectional base vectors n1 and n2 are assumed to remain normal to the beam axis, so  d⁢xd⁢S⋅n1=d⁢xd⁢S⋅n2=0.  Hence,  d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n1d⁢S=-d2⁢xd⁢S2⋅n1,    and    d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢n2d⁢S=-d2⁢xd⁢S2⋅n2.  So we have  K2=G-1⁢(n1⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2-(1+ε)⁢N1⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2)  and  K1=-G-1⁢(n2⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2-(1+ε)⁢N2⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2).  This defines the generalized strains associated with axial stretch. For torsional strain the internal virtual work rate is  δ⁢W2I=∫L∫Aγαt⁢ταf⁢δ⁢e1⁢d⁢A⁢d⁢L,  where  e1=n2⋅d⁢n1d⁢S-N2⋅d⁢N1d⁢S  and γαt is the proportionality constant between shear strains and torsional strains (see [Beam element formulation](simathe-c-beamform.md) for details). For computational simplicity the form of the torsional strains is taken to be  e1=n2⋅d⁢n~1d⁢S-N2⋅d⁢N1d⁢S,  where  n~1=C⁢(p1⁢ϕ1+p2⁢ϕ2)⋅N1,  and  p1​=12⁢(1-ξ),p2​=12⁢(1+ξ),-1≤ξ≤1.  This assumes a linear interpolation of the finite rotation vector, ϕ, along the beam. Thus, the generalized strain measures for these beams are  ε  axial strain,  K1 and K2  the beam curvature change measures, and  e1  the torsional strain.  With these measures, the internal virtual work rate can be written  δ⁢WI=∫L[δ⁢ε⁢∫Aσf⁢d⁢A-δ⁢K2⁢∫AS1⁢σf⁢d⁢A+δ⁢K1⁢∫AS2⁢σf⁢d⁢A+δ⁢e1⁢∫Aγαt⁢ταf⁢d⁢A]⁢d⁢L.  Internal virtual work rate Jacobian For the Jacobian matrix of the overall Newton method, [Equation 3](simathe-c-procedures.md#simathe-c-procedures-t-BasicFiniteElementEquations-sma-topic1__simathe-c-procedures-eq3), the variation of this internal work rate with respect to nodal displacement variations must be formed. The constitutive theory is written as  {d⁢σfd⁢τf}=[H]{d⁢εfd⁢γf},  and so  dδWI=∫L[⌊δε    δK1    δK2    δe1⌋[    B    ]{d⁢εd⁢K1d⁢K2d⁢e1}+dδε∫AσfdA+dδK1∫AS2σfdA  -dδK2∫AS1σfdA+dδe1∫AγαfταfdA]dL,  where [B] is the section stiffness matrix obtained by integration over the cross-section. See [Beam element formulation](simathe-c-beamform.md) for more information on section integration.  First variations of strains Taking the variations of the above strain definitions gives directly  δ⁢ε​=G-1⁢d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S,δ⁢K2​=G-1⁢(δ⁢n1⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2+n1⋅d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2-δ⁢ε⁢N1⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2)δ⁢K1​=G-1⁢(δ⁢n2⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2+n2⋅d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2-δ⁢ε⁢N2⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2)  andδ⁢e1​=δ⁢n2⋅d⁢n~1d⁢S+n2⋅d⁢δ⁢n~1d⁢S.  In these expressions we need δ⁢n1 and δ⁢n2 as well as δ⁢n~1; these are now derived. From the expression for n~1, namely  n~1=C⁢(p1⁢ϕ1+p2⁢ϕ2)⋅N1,  another ancillary vector n¯1, normal to the tangent, is defined by  n¯1=n~1-n~1⋅tt  so that  n1=n¯1/|n¯1|.  In addition,  t=g-12⁢d⁢xd⁢S  where  g=d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢xd⁢S  so that  δ⁢t=g-12⁢(I-tt)⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S.  Since (t1n1n2) form an orthonormal triad,  (2)δ⁢n1​=δ⁢n1⋅(n2⁢n2+tt)​=δ⁢n1⋅n2⁢n2+δ⁢n1⋅tt​=δ⁢n1⋅n2⁢n2-g-12⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢t,  because n1⋅t=0. From the definition of n1, it is straightforward to show that  δ⁢n1=1|n¯1|⁢(I-n1⁢n1)⋅δ⁢n¯.  So  δ⁢n1⋅n2​=1|n¯1|⁢n2⋅δ⁢n1​=1|n¯1|⁢n2⋅[δ⁢n~1-(δ⁢n~1⋅t)⁢t-(n~1⋅t)⁢δ⁢t]​=1|n¯1|⁢[n2⋅δ⁢n~1-g-12⁢(n~1⋅t)⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S]  and  δ⁢n~1=δ⁢ω×n~1⁢.  Thus,  δ⁢n1⋅n2=1|n¯1|⁢(δ⁢ω⋅n~1×n2-g-12⁢n~1⋅tn2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S).  We can also write  n~1×n2=n~1⁢t-n~1⋅t⁢n1  and  |n¯1|=n¯1⋅n1=n~1⋅n1.  Combining terms appropriately,  δ⁢n1⋅n2=δ⁢ω⋅t-n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1⁢[δ⁢ω⋅n1+g-12⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S].  Hence, from [Equation 2](simathe-c-eulerbernbeams.md#simathe-c-eulerbernbeams-t-FirstVariationsOfStrains-sma-topic3__simathe-c-eulerbernbeams-eq2)  δ⁢n1=δ⁢ω⋅tn2-n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1⁢[δ⁢ω⋅n1+g-12⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S]⁢n2-g-12⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢t.  In a similar manner one can show that  δ⁢n2=-δ⁢ω⋅tn1-n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1⁢[δ⁢ω⋅n1+g-12⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S]⁢n1-g-12⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢t.  The first variations of strain become  δ⁢ε​=G-1⁢d⁢xd⁢S⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢Sδ⁢K2=G-1[n2⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2(t-n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1n1)⋅δω-g-12⁢(t⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢n1+n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1⁢n2⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2)⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S+n1d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2-δεN1⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2]δ⁢K1=G-1[n1⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2(t-n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1n1)⋅δω+g-12⁢(t⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢n1+n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1⁢n1⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2)⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S-n2d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2-δεN2⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2]andδ⁢e1​=(d⁢n~1d⁢S⋅n1⁢n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1-d⁢n~1d⁢S⁢t)⁢n1⋅δ⁢ω+g-12⁢[n~1⋅tn~1⋅n1⁢d⁢n~1d⁢S⋅n1-d⁢n~1d⁢S⋅t]⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S+n~1×n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ωd⁢S  so that  n1=n¯1/|n¯1|.  In addition,  t=g-12⁢d⁢xd⁢S  so that  δ⁢t=g-12⁢(I-tt)⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S,  since (t1    n1    n2) form an orthonormal triad,  δ⁢n1​=δ⁢n1⋅(n2⁢n2+tt)​=δ⁢n1⋅n2⁢n2+δ⁢n1⋅tt​=δ⁢n1⋅n2⁢n2-g-12⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢t,  because  n1⋅t=0.  Second variations of strains The second variation of the axial strain is simply  d⁢δ⁢ε=G-1⁢d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⋅d⁢d⁢ud⁢S.  To compute the second variations of bending strain, we need expressions for d⁢δ⁢n1,d⁢δ⁢n2. These are obtained by approximating  δ⁢n1⁢​≈n2⁢t⋅δ⁢ω-t⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢Sδ⁢n2​≈-n1⁢t⋅δ⁢ω-t⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢Sδ⁢t​≈δ⁢ω⋅n2n1-δ⁢ω⋅n1⁢n2  from which  d⁢δ⁢n1=d⁢n2⁢t⋅δ⁢ω+n2⁢d⁢t⋅δ⁢ω-d⁢t⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S-t⁢d⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S.  In the above approximations we have assumed that the following conditions are satisfied  n˜1⋅t≪1⁢, n˜2⋅t≪1,  and⁢  ⁢g≈1.  Using these expressions, the second variations of bending strains are written as  d⁢δ⁢K1=​G-1[n2⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2t⋅dωt⋅δω-t⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢(n1⋅d⁢d⁢ud⁢S⁢t⋅δ⁢ω+n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢t⋅d⁢ω)+n1⋅d2⁢d⁢ud⁢S2⁢t⋅δ⁢ω+t⋅d2⁢d⁢ud⁢S2⁢n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S+n1⋅d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2⁢t⋅d⁢ω+t⋅d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2⁢n2⋅d⁢d⁢ud⁢S+d⁢δ⁢ε⁢N2⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2+n1⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢(n2⋅d⁢ω⁢n1⋅δ⁢ω-n1⋅d⁢ω⁢n2⋅δ⁢ω+n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢n2⋅d⁢ω)-n2⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢(n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢n1⋅d⁢ω)-t⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2(n2⋅d⁢d⁢ud⁢Sn2t⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S)]  and  d⁢δ⁢K2=G-1[-n1⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2t⋅δωt⋅dω-t⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢(n2⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢t⋅d⁢ω+n2⋅d⁢d⁢ud⁢S⁢t⋅δ⁢ω)+n2⋅d2⁢d⁢ud⁢S2⁢t⋅δ⁢ω-t⋅d2⁢d⁢ud⁢S2⁢n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S+n2⋅d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2⁢t⋅d⁢ω-t⋅d2⁢δ⁢ud⁢S2⁢n1⋅d⁢d⁢ud⁢S-d⁢δ⁢ε⁢N1⋅d2⁢Xd⁢S2+n2⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢(n2⋅d⁢ω⁢n1⋅δ⁢ω-n1⋅d⁢ω⁢n2⋅δ⁢ω+n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢n1⋅d⁢ω)-n1⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2⁢(n1⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S⁢n2⋅d⁢ω)-t⋅d2⁢xd⁢S2(n1⋅d⁢d⁢ud⁢Sn1t⋅d⁢δ⁢ud⁢S)].  For the torsional strain contribution to the initial stress matrix, we approximate  δ⁢e2​≈t⋅d⁢δ⁢ωd⁢Sd⁢δ⁢e1≈dt⋅d⁢δ⁢ωd⁢S=dω×t⋅d⁢δ⁢ωd⁢S.  This matrix is again unsymmetric.  Interpolation In the virtual work equation the strains include second derivatives of displacement. For this reason continuity of rotation as well as displacement is needed so that the Hermitian polynomial interpolation functions are the minimum interpolation order needed. These are used here. The Hermite cubic is written in terms of the function value and its derivative at the ends of the interval:  u(ξ)=N1(ξ)u1+N2(ξ)u2+N3(ξ)d⁢u1d⁢ξ+N4(ξ)d⁢u2d⁢ξ        ,-1<ξ<1,  with node 1 at ξ=-1 and node 2 at ξ=+1.  These functions are used in Abaqus to interpolate the components of displacement u and the initial position vector x, so that the elements are basically isoparametric. In addition, rotation of (n1n2) about the beam axis, ϕa, is interpolated linearly. This interpolation is unsatisfactory for the user, because the nodal variables are  (ux,uy,uz,d⁢uxd⁢S,d⁢uyd⁢S,d⁢uzd⁢S,ϕa).  The last four of these variables are difficult to work with; furthermore, making them the same in all elements sharing the same node causes excessive constraint of axial stretch in these elements, especially if the beam axis is not continuous through the node, as in a frame structure or “T” junction.  To avoid this difficulty, the following procedure is adopted. At a node the tangent to the beam axis is  t=d⁢xd⁢l=d⁢xd⁢S⁢d⁢Sd⁢l,  so  d⁢xd⁢S=d⁢ld⁢S⁢t.  Now suppose we store as degrees of freedom at the node,  (ux,uy,uz,ϕx,ϕy,ϕz,d⁢l/d⁢S),  where (ϕx,ϕy,ϕz) are the x-, y- and z-components of the finite rotation vector, ϕ. Since the initial geometry and hence T, the initial direction of the beam axis, is known, t=C⋅T, where C is the rotation matrix defined by ϕ, and hence  (3)d⁢xd⁢S=d⁢ld⁢S⁢C⋅T  is defined by these variables and the initial geometry. Furthermore, ϕa is directly available from ϕ and t. Thus, the above set is a satisfactory set of nodal variables. To eliminate the unwanted axial strain constraint, in Abaqus the stretch d⁢l/d⁢S at the node of each such element is taken as an internal variable, local to the element (a third internal node is created for this purpose, and so it is not shared with neighboring elements.)  It should be remarked that the above transformation ([Equation 3](simathe-c-eulerbernbeams.md#simathe-c-eulerbernbeams-t-Interpolation-sma-topic5__simathe-c-eulerbernbeams-eq3)) is nonlinear. This leads to some complications—for example, the d'Alembert forces no longer have the simple form MM⁢N⁢u¯¨N; rather, a matrix M¯M⁢N=QM⁢P⁢MP⁢Q⁢QQ⁢N replaces MM⁢N where QM⁢P and QQ⁢N use the transformation ([Equation 3](simathe-c-eulerbernbeams.md#simathe-c-eulerbernbeams-t-Interpolation-sma-topic5__simathe-c-eulerbernbeams-eq3)) and its appropriate time derivatives. The resulting Jacobian is nonsymmetric; Abaqus ignores the nonsymmetric terms.  Integration The cross-section integration has already been discussed in the context of the shear beams—it is the same for these beams. Along the beam axis, the integration schemes are as described below.  Stiffness and internal forces  Three Gauss points are used. Two Gauss points are not sufficient because the torsional strain is not independent.  Mass and distributed loads  Three Gauss points are used. Rotary inertia is not included in the mass, except for rotation of the section about the beam axis, where it is included to avoid singularity in perfectly straight beams. |
