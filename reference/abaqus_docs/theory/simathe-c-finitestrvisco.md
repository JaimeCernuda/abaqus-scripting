In the above, τ0D and τ0H are, respectively, the deviatoric and the hydrostatic parts of the instantaneous Kirchhoff stress τ0. F¯ is the “distortion gradient” related to the deformation gradient F by  F¯=FJ13,  where  J=det⁢(F)  is the volume change.  Using integration by parts and a variable transformation, the basic hereditary integral formulation for linear isotropic viscoelasticity can be written in the form  σ⁢(t)=2⁢G0⁢e⁢(t)+∫0τ2⁢G˙⁢(τ′)⁢e⁢(t-t′)⁢d⁢τ′+I⁢(K0⁢ϕ⁢(t)+∫0τK˙⁢(τ′)⁢ϕ⁢(t-t′)⁢d⁢τ′)  or entirely in terms of stress quantities,  σ⁢(t)=S0⁢(t)+∫0τG˙⁢(τ′)G0⁢S0⁢(t-t′)⁢d⁢τ′+I⁢(p0⁢(t)+∫0τK˙⁢(τ′)K0⁢p⁢(t-t′)⁢d⁢τ′),  where τ is the reduced time, G˙⁢(τ′)=d⁢G⁢(τ′)/d⁢τ′, and K˙⁢(τ′)=d⁢K⁢(τ′)/d⁢τ′. G0 and K0 are the instantaneous small-strain shear and bulk moduli, and G⁢(t) and K⁢(t) are the time-dependent small-strain shear and bulk relaxation moduli. Recall that the reduced time represents a shift in time with temperature and is related to the actual time through the differential equation  d⁢τ′=d⁢t′Aθ⁢(θ⁢(t′)),  where θ is the temperature and Aθ is the shift function.  Using the volumetric/deviatoric-split hereditary integral in the reference configuration for large strain (hyperelastic) materials, and then using a standard push-forward operator (see [Simo, 1987](simathe-c-references.md#simathe-c-references__simathe-c-ref-simo-1987)), one obtains the following set of equations in the current configuration:  (1)τD⁢(t)​=τ0D⁢(t)+dev⁢[∫0τG˙⁢(τ′)G0⁢F¯t-1⁢(t-t′)⋅τ0D⁢(t-t′)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t-t′)⁢d⁢τ′],τH⁢(t)​=τ0H⁢(t)+∫0τK˙⁢(τ′)K0⁢τ0H⁢(t-t′)⁢d⁢τ′.  where dev⁢(∙)=(∙)-13⁢(∙):I⁢I and F¯t⁢(t-t′) is the distortional deformation gradient of the state at t-t′ relative to the state at t . A transformation is performed on the stress relating the state at time t-t′ to the state at time t.  Implementation As in small-strain viscoelasticity, we represent the relaxation moduli in terms of the Prony series  (2)G⁢(τ)=G0⁢(g∞+∑i=1NGgi⁢e-τ/τiG),  (3)K⁢(τ)=K0⁢(k∞+∑i=1NKki⁢e-τ/τiK),  where gi and ki are the relative moduli of terms i. Note that g∞+∑i=1NGgi=k∞+∑i=1NKki=1. Abaqus assumes that the relaxation times τi=τiK=τiG are the same so that from here on, we will sum on N terms for both bulk and shear behavior. In reality, the number of nonzero terms in bulk and shear, NK and NG, need not be equal, unless the instantaneous behavior is based on the hyperfoam model. In the latter case, the two deformation modes are closely related and are then assumed to relax equally and simultaneously.  Substituting [Equation 2](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-Implementation-sma-topic2__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq2) and [Equation 3](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-Implementation-sma-topic2__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq3) in [Equation 1](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegralFormulation-sma-topic1__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq1), we obtain  (4)τD⁢(t)​=τ0D⁢(t)-dev⁢[∑i=1Ngiτi⁢∫0τF¯t-1⁢(t-t′)⋅τ0D⁢(t-t′)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t-t′)⁢e-τ′τi⁢d⁢τ′],τH⁢(t)​=τ0H⁢(t)-∑i=1Nkiτi⁢∫0ττ0H⁢(t-t′)⁢e-τ′τi⁢d⁢τ′.  Next, we introduce the internal stresses, associated with each term of the series  (5)τiD⁢(t)≡giτi⁢∫0τF¯t-1⁢(t-t′)⋅τ0D⁢(t-t′)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t-t′)⁢e-τ′τi⁢d⁢τ′,  (6)τiH⁢(t)≡kiτi⁢∫0ττ0H⁢(t-t′)⁢e-τ′τi⁢d⁢τ′.  These stresses are stored at each material point and are integrated forward in time. We will assume that the solution is known at time t, and we need to construct the solution at time t+Δ⁢t.  Integration of the hydrostatic stress The internal hydrostatic stresses τiH at time t+Δ⁢t follow from  τiH⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=kiτi⁢∫0τ+Δ⁢ττ0H⁢(t+Δ⁢t-t′)⁢e-τ′τi⁢d⁢τ′.  With τ¯=τ′-Δ⁢τ and t¯=t′-Δ⁢t, it follows that  τiH⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=​kiτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫-Δ⁢τττ0H⁢(t-t¯)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯=kiτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫-Δ⁢τ0τ0H⁢(t-t¯)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯+kiτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫0ττ0H⁢(t-t¯)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯,  which yields with [Equation 6](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-Implementation-sma-topic2__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq6)  (7)τiH⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=kiτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫-Δ⁢τ0τ0H⁢(t-t¯)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯+e-Δ⁢ττi⁢τiH⁢(t).  To integrate the first integral in [Equation 7](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheHydrostaticStress-sma-topic3__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq7), we assume that τ0H⁢(t-t¯) varies linearly with the reduced time τ¯ over the increment  (8)τ0H⁢(t-t¯)=(1+τ¯Δ⁢τ)⁢τ0H⁢(t)-τ¯Δ⁢τ⁢τ0H⁢(t+Δ⁢t)        -Δ⁢τ≤τ¯≤0.  Substituting into [Equation 7](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheHydrostaticStress-sma-topic3__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq7) yields  τiH⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=​[kiτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫-Δ⁢τ0-τ¯Δ⁢τ⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯]⁢τ0H⁢(t+Δ⁢t)+[kiτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫-Δ⁢τ0(1+τ¯Δ⁢τ)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯]⁢τ0H⁢(t)+e-Δ⁢ττi⁢τiH⁢(t).  The integrals are readily evaluated, providing the solution at the end of the increment  τiH⁢(t+Δ⁢t)​=[1-τiΔ⁢τ⁢(1-e-Δ⁢ττi)]⁢ki⁢τ0H⁢(t+Δ⁢t)+[τiΔ⁢τ⁢(1-e-Δ⁢ττi)-e-Δ⁢ττi]⁢ki⁢τ0H⁢(t)+e-Δ⁢ττi⁢τiH⁢(t)  or, in a slightly different form  (9)τiH⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=αi⁢ki⁢τ0H⁢(t+Δ⁢t)+βi⁢ki⁢τ0H⁢(t)+γi⁢τiH⁢(t),  with  γi=e-Δ⁢ττi,    αi=1-τiΔ⁢τ⁢(1-γi),    βi=τiΔ⁢τ⁢(1-γi)-γi.  Observe that for Δ⁢t=Δ⁢τ=0, γi=1 and αi=βi=0. For Δ⁢t=Δ⁢τ=∞, αi=1 and γi=βi=0.  Integration of the deviatoric stress The internal deviatoric stresses τiD at time t+Δ⁢t follow from  (10)τiD(t+Δ⁢t)=​giτi⁢∫0τ+Δ⁢τF¯t+Δ⁢t-1⁢(t+Δ⁢t-t′)⋅τ0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t-t′)⋅F¯t+Δ⁢t-T⁢(t+Δ⁢t-t′)⁢e-τ′τi⁢d⁢τ′.  Observe that  F¯t+Δ⁢t⁢(t-t′)=F¯t⁢(t-t′)⋅F¯t+Δ⁢t⁢(τ),  and the inverse of this is  F¯t+Δ⁢t-1⁢(t-t′)=F¯t+Δ⁢t-1⁢(t)⋅F¯t-1⁢(t-t′)=F¯t⁢(t+Δ⁢t)⋅F¯t-1⁢(t-t′),  which—when substituted into [Equation 10](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq10) with Δ⁢F¯≡F¯t⁢(t+Δ⁢t) and Δ⁢F¯-1≡F¯t+Δ⁢t⁢(t)—gives  τiD(t+Δ⁢t)=​giτi⁢Δ⁢F¯⋅∫0τ+Δ⁢τF¯t-1⁢(t+Δ⁢t-t′)⋅τ0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t-t′)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t+Δ⁢t-t′)⁢e-τ′τi⁢d⁢τ′⋅Δ⁢F¯T.  With τ¯=τ′-Δ⁢τ and t¯=t′-Δ⁢t, it follows:  (11)τiD⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=​giτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢Δ⁢F¯⋅∫-Δ⁢ττF¯t-1⁢(t-t¯)⋅τ0D⁢(t-t¯)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t-t¯)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯⋅Δ⁢F¯T=giτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫-Δ⁢τ0Δ⁢F¯⋅F¯t-1⁢(t-t¯)⋅τ0D⁢(t-t¯)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t-t¯)⋅Δ⁢F¯T⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯+giτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢Δ⁢F¯⋅∫0τF¯t-1⁢(t-t¯)⋅τ0D⁢(t-t¯)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t-t¯)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯⋅Δ⁢F¯T.  Now introduce the variable  (12)τ^0D⁢(t-t¯)≡Δ⁢F¯⋅F¯t-1⁢(t-t¯)⋅τ0D⁢(t-t¯)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t-t¯)⋅Δ⁢F¯T.  Note that  (13)τ^0D⁢(t)=Δ⁢F¯⋅F¯t-1⁢(t)⋅τ0D⁢(t)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t)⋅Δ⁢F¯T=Δ⁢F¯⋅τ0D⁢(t)⋅Δ⁢F¯T  and  (14)τ^0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=Δ⁢F¯⋅F¯t-1⁢(t+Δ⁢t)⋅τ0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t)⋅F¯t-T⁢(t+Δ⁢t)⋅Δ⁢F¯T=τ0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t).  Then we can also introduce  (15)τ^iD⁢(t)=Δ⁢F¯⋅τiD⁢(t)⋅Δ⁢F¯T.  Substitution of [Equation 5](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-Implementation-sma-topic2__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq5), [Equation 12](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq12), and [Equation 15](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq15) into [Equation 11](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq11) yields  (16)τiD⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=giτi⁢e-Δ⁢ττi⁢∫-Δ⁢τ0τ^0D⁢(t-t¯)⁢e-τ¯τi⁢d⁢τ¯+e-Δ⁢ττi⁢τ^iD⁢(t).  To integrate the first integral in [Equation 16](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq16), we assume that τ^0D⁢(t-t¯) varies linearly with the reduced time τ¯ over the increment:  τ^0D⁢(t-t¯)=(1+τ¯Δ⁢τ)⁢τ^0D⁢(t)-τ¯Δ⁢τ⁢τ^0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t)                -Δ⁢τ≤τ¯≤0,  which with [Equation 14](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq14) becomes  (17)τ^0D⁢(t-t¯)=(1+τ¯Δ⁢τ)⁢τ^0D⁢(t)-τ¯Δ⁢τ⁢τ0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t)                -Δ⁢τ≤τ¯≤0. The total stress at the end of the increment becomes  (19)τ⁢(t+Δ⁢t)=τ0⁢(t+Δ⁢t)-∑i=1NτiD⁢(t+Δ⁢t)-∑i=1NτiH⁢(t+Δ⁢t),  which with [Equation 9](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheHydrostaticStress-sma-topic3__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq9) and [Equation 18](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq18) can also be written as  (20)τ⁢(t+Δ⁢t)​=(1-∑i=1Nαi⁢gi)⁢τ0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t)+(1-∑i=1Nαi⁢ki)⁢τ0H⁢(t+Δ⁢t)-∑i=1Nβi⁢gi⁢dev⁢(τ^0D⁢(t))-∑i=1Nβi⁢ki⁢τ0H⁢(t)-∑i=1Nγi⁢dev⁢(τ^iD⁢(t))-∑i=1Nγi⁢τiH⁢(t).  Rate equation To solve the system of nonlinear equations generated by the constitutive equations, we need to generate the corotational constitutive rate equations. From [Equation 20](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq20) it follows  (21)τˇ⁢(t+Δ⁢t)​=(1-∑i=1Nαi⁢gi)⁢τˇ0D⁢(t+Δ⁢t)+(1-∑i=1Nαi⁢ki)⁢τˇ0H⁢(t+Δ⁢t)-        ∑i=1Nβigidev(τ^ˇ0D(t))-∑i=1Nγidev(τ^ˇiD(t)),  where τˇ⁢(t) is the corotational (Jaumann) stress rate. The rate form of the above equation is used to compute the Jacobian.  Cauchy versus Kirchhoff stress All equations have been worked out in terms of the Kirchhoff stress. However, the implementation in Abaqus uses the Cauchy stress. To transform to Cauchy stress, we use the relations  S⁢(t)​=τD⁢(t)/J⁢(t),p⁢(t)=-13I:τH(t)/J(t).  With Δ⁢J≡J⁢(t+Δ⁢t)/J⁢(t), this allows us to write [Equation 9](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheHydrostaticStress-sma-topic3__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq9), [Equation 13](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq13), [Equation 15](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq15), [Equation 18](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq18), and [Equation 19](simathe-c-finitestrvisco.md#simathe-c-finitestrvisco-t-IntegrationOfTheDeviatoricStress-sma-topic4__simathe-c-finitestrvisco-eq19) in the following form:  pi⁢(t+Δ⁢t)​=αi⁢ki⁢p0⁢(t+Δ⁢t)+βi⁢ki⁢p0⁢(t)+γi⁢pi⁢(t)Δ⁢J,S^0⁢(t)​=Δ⁢F¯⋅S0⁢(t)⋅Δ⁢F¯T,S^i⁢(t)​=Δ⁢F¯⋅Si⁢(t)⋅Δ⁢F¯T,Si⁢(t+Δ⁢t)​=αi⁢gi⁢S0⁢(t+Δ⁢t)+βi⁢gi⁢S^0⁢(t)+γi⁢S^i⁢(t)Δ⁢J,  σ⁢(t+Δ⁢t)​=σ0⁢(t+Δ⁢t)-∑i=1Ndev⁢(Si⁢(t+Δ⁢t))+∑i=1Npi⁢(t+Δ⁢t)⁢I.  The virtual work and rate of virtual work equations are written with respect to the current volume. Therefore, the corotational stress rates are rates of Kirchhoff stress mapped into the current configuration and transformed in the same way as the stresses themselves.  This set of equations—combined with the expressions for αi, βi, and γi—describe the full implementation of the hyper-viscoelasticity model in a displacement formulation.  The rate equations can be written in a form similar to [Hyperelastic material behavior](simathe-c-hyperelastic.md). Introduce  CvS=(1-∑i=1Nαi⁢gi)⁢C0S  and  Kv=(1-∑i=1Nαi⁢ki)⁢K0,  where C0S and K0 are the instantaneous moduli, corresponding to CS and K of [Hyperelastic material behavior](simathe-c-hyperelastic.md). Thus, all rate equations can be obtained by substitution of C0S by CvS and K0 by Kv. |
