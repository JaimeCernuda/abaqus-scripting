If the interface permeability is considered infinite (i.e., no resistance to fluid flow), it is required at all times that the pore pressures on opposite sides be equal:  p2-p1=0.  Similarly, let q1 and q2 be the volume flow rate densities normal to the interface at the two sides, and let h˙=v2-v1=n⋅(v2-v1) be the relative velocity of the two sides in the direction of the interface normal.  It is assumed that the interface is filled with fluid up to a separation threshold. Hence, continuity requires that  q1+  q2=-h˙⁢    for-h<dt⁢h⁢r⁢e⁢s⁢h⁢o⁢l⁢d,q1+  q2=0             for-h≥dt⁢h⁢r⁢e⁢s⁢h⁢o⁢l⁢d,  whereas the difference corresponds to twice the flow across the interface.  q2-q1=2⁢qa⁢c⁢r⁢o⁢s⁢s  is undetermined and is to be treated as an independent variable in the case of infinite permeability. Inversion of these equations yields  q1=​-12⁢h˙-qa⁢c⁢r⁢o⁢s⁢s,q2=​-12⁢h˙+qa⁢c⁢r⁢o⁢s⁢s.  The pore fluid constraints on the contact interface for finite interface permeability For finite gap permeability, k, the volume flow rate density directly across the interface is  qa⁢c⁢r⁢o⁢s⁢s=k⁢(p2-p1).  It is assumed that the interface is filled with fluid up to a separation threshold. Hence, continuity requires that  q1+q2=-h˙⁢for-h<dt⁢h⁢r⁢e⁢s⁢h⁢o⁢l⁢d,q1+q2=0    for⁢h≥dt⁢h⁢r⁢e⁢s⁢h⁢o⁢l⁢d.  Volume flow rate densities normal to the interface at the two sides of the interface are  q1​=-η⁢k1k1+k2⁢h˙-qa⁢c⁢r⁢o⁢s⁢s,q2​=-η⁢k2k1+k2⁢h˙+qa⁢c⁢r⁢o⁢s⁢s,  where  η=1    if-h<dt⁢h⁢r⁢e⁢s⁢h⁢o⁢l⁢d,η=0    if-h≥dt⁢h⁢r⁢e⁢s⁢h⁢o⁢l⁢d.  k1 and k2 are underlying material permeabilities.  The transient equations The contribution of the interfacial virtual work equation and its linearized form are first obtained in the general form including finite sliding. The equations are then specialized to the various formulations implemented in Abaqus.  Since we want to achieve force and volume flow rate equilibrium at each side of the interface, as well as obtain continuity in the pore pressures, we add the following integral to the virtual work equation:  δ⁢Πi⁢p⁢p=∫A[q1⁢δ⁢p1+q2⁢δ⁢p2-p2⁢δ⁢v2+p1⁢δ⁢v1-(p1-p2)⁢δ⁢λ]⁢d⁢A,  where δ⁢λ is an arbitrary Lagrange multiplier and A is the interface area. Eliminating q1 and q2 and using a suitable choice for δ⁢λ,  δ⁢λ=12⁢(δ⁢q^+δ⁢v2+δ⁢v1),  we obtain  δ⁢Πi⁢p⁢p=-∫A[h˙⁢δ⁢p¯+p¯⁢δ⁢h˙+q^⁢δ⁢p^+p^⁢δ⁢q^]⁢d⁢A,  where  p¯=p1+p22;    p^=p1-p22;    δ⁢p¯=δ⁢p1+δ⁢p22;    δ⁢p^=δ⁢p1-δ⁢p22.  Since Abaqus uses displacements (not velocities) and fluxes integrated over Δ⁢t, the equation can be multiplied by Δ⁢t to obtain  δ⁢Πi⁢p⁢p=-∫A[Δ⁢h⁢δ⁢p¯+p¯⁢δ⁢h+Δ⁢t⁢(q^⁢δ⁢p^+p^⁢δ⁢q^)]⁢d⁢A,  where Δ⁢h is the incremental change in h in the direction of the interface normal. Linearization yields  dδΠi⁢p⁢p=-∫A[dhδp¯+Δhdδp¯+dp¯δh+p¯dδh+Δt(dq^δp^+q^dδp^+dp^δq^+p^dδq^)]dA.  Closed contact If the two sides are locally in contact, Δ⁢h=0 and d=0; therefore, the virtual work simplifies to  δ⁢Πi⁢p⁢p=-∫A[p¯⁢δ⁢h+Δ⁢t⁢(q^⁢δ⁢p^+p^⁢δ⁢q^)]⁢d⁢A.  Similarly, the linearized form simplifies to  d⁢δ⁢Πi⁢p⁢p=-∫A[d⁢p¯⁢δ⁢h+p¯⁢d⁢δ⁢h+Δ⁢t⁢(d⁢q^⁢δ⁢p^+q^⁢d⁢δ⁢p^+d⁢p^⁢δ⁢q^+p^⁢d⁢δ⁢q^)]⁢d⁢A.  The steady-state equations For steady-state analysis the transient terms can be omitted, and the terms involving fluid flow are written in rate form. In this case we can assume that the interface displacements vanish, which leads to the simplified virtual work contribution  δ⁢Πs⁢si⁢p⁢p=-∫A[(p¯⁢δ⁢h+q^⁢δ⁢p^+p^⁢δ⁢q^)]⁢d⁢A  and the linearized form  d⁢δ⁢Πs⁢si⁢p⁢p=-∫A[d⁢p¯⁢δ⁢h+p¯⁢d⁢δ⁢h+d⁢q^⁢δ⁢p^+q^⁢d⁢δ⁢p^+d⁢p^⁢δ⁢q^+p^⁢d⁢δ⁢q^]⁢d⁢A.  Small sliding When the small-sliding contact formulation is used, the terms d⁢δ⁢p¯, p¯⁢d⁢δ⁢h, d⁢δ⁢p^, and p^⁢d⁢δ⁢q^ in the linearized virtual work equation will vanish. |
