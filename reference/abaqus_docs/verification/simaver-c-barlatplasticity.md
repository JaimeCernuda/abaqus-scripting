Problem description This verification test contains a set of single-element models subject to uniaxial tension for various off-axis angles between the material directions and the loading direction. The test is designed to verify the yield stress ratios and strain ratios (Lankford's r-values) obtained in the simulations against the analytical solutions. Model  [Figure 1](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-figuresect__simaver-c-ver-mat-barlat-model) shows the elements used in the analysis. All elements are subject to uniaxial tensile loading in the global x-direction. Each element has a unique material orientation. The material orientation is assigned such that for the three rows of elements (from bottom to top), the loading direction lies in the material 1-2 plane, 2-3 plane, 3-1 plane, respectively; and for the seven columns of elements (from left to right), the off-axis angle of loading is 0°, 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, 90°, respectively. Four materials with Barlat anisotropy coefficients are tested. Coefficients of the Barlat Yld2004-18p yield surface for material 2090-T3 and 6111-T4 are shown in [Table 1](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-tablesect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-tab-barlat). The yield surfaces of the material 2090-T3 are illustrated in [Figure 2](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-figuresect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-ver-mat-barlat-yldsurf-2090t3-xy), [Figure 3](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-figuresect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-ver-mat-barlat-yldsurf-2090t3-yz), and [Figure 4](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-figuresect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-ver-mat-barlat-yldsurf-2090t3-zx) for three possible plane stress conditions, respectively. The other two materials, 2024-T3 and 2008-T4, use the Barlat Yld91 yield surface with anisotropy coefficients as shown in [Table 2](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-tablesect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-tab-barlat91).  In addition, the Mises isotropic plasticity is recovered by using an equivalent definition for the Barlat yield surfaces.  Results and discussion The yield stress ratio is defined as  R =     σ   x       σ   0     ,  where      σ   x     is the stress in the loading direction that can be obtained as output variable S11 in the global frame and      σ   0     is the reference yield stress that can be obtained as output variable YIELDPOT.  The strain ratio (Lankford's r-value) is defined as  r =     ε   y       ε   z     ,  where      ε   y     and      ε   z     are the plastic strains in the width and thickness directions, respectively, corresponding to output variables PE22 and PE33 in the global frame.  The results of the yield stress and strain ratios agree well with the analytical solutions for the four materials. Comparisons of the simulation results and analytical solutions for the material 2090-T3 with loading applied on three material planes are shown in [Figure 5](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-figuresect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-ver-mat-barlat-2090t3-xy), [Figure 6](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-figuresect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-ver-mat-barlat-2090t3-yz), and [Figure 7](simaver-c-barlatplasticity.md#simaver-c-barlatplasticity-figuresect__simaver-c-barlatplasticity-ver-mat-barlat-2090t3-zx).  The yield stress ratio and strain ratio of the Mises plasticity are always one. The models with equivalent definitions of the Mises yield surface also match the analytical solutions.
