Elements tested FP3D2AT  FP3D2T  Problem description Thermal fluid pipe elements are used to model fluid flow in a vertical wellbore. Two scenarios of laminar and turbulent flows are modeled in the wellbore. Point A is located 2000 m below ground level, and point B is located at ground level. The diameter of the pipe is 0.1 m. Gravity effects are not included in this model. Four different viscosity definitions (Newtonian, Herschel-Bulkley, Bingham plastic, and power law) are used to verify that the computed pressures match the analytical solution. The temperature evolution due to convective effects at the fluid–pipe wall interface is modeled using either a constant temperature approach or a constant flux approach.  Material: The fluid is modeled as an incompressible liquid with a density of 1000 kg/m3:  * Newtonian fluid: viscosity defined to be 0.001 Pa∙sec * Herschel-Bulkley fluid: shear viscosity at low shear rates of 1 ×   10-5 Pa∙sec, a yield shear stress of 1 × 10-3   Pa, a consistency index of 0.001, and a flow behavior index of 0.8 * Bingham plastic fluid: shear viscosity at the low shear rate of 1 ×   10-3 Pa∙sec, a yield shear stress of 0.01 Pa, and a   consistency index of 0.001 * Power law fluid: consistency index of 0.001, a flow behavior index of   0.8, a maximum viscosity of 1 × 107 Pa∙sec, and a minimum   viscosity of 1 × 10-6 Pa∙sec  Loading: For laminar flow, an inlet fluid flow rate of 5 × 10−4 m3/s at point B and a pressure of 0 MPa at point A are prescribed. For turbulent flow, an inlet flow rate of 1.5 × 10−3 m3/s at point B and a pressure of 0 MPa at point A are specified. For the thermal solution, the fluid temperature is prescribed to be 100 and the outer wall temperature is prescribed to be 10.  Reference solution Since the fluid is incompressible, the pressures can be computed using the Bernoulli equation for pipe flow.  Results and discussion The analytical pressures computed using the Bernoulli equation for pipe flow at point B are shown in the table below. Since the prescribed boundary conditions for the laminar and turbulent flows are different, the computed pressures are different for the two cases.
