Finite element models of a repeating unit cell use the same constituent properties and microstructure of the composite as the mean-field model. Periodic boundary conditions are applied on the boundary nodes of the unit cell using the following equation: u  i  (   x  j +   p  j  α ) =   u  i  (   x  j ) +  〈   u  i , k 〉   p  k  α , where      u  i  is the nodal displacement,      p  j  α  is the      α  t h  vector defining the periodicity of the unit cell, and     〈   u  i , k 〉  is the far-field displacement gradient, which is assumed to be equal to the nominal strain given that the far-field rotation is constrained to be zero. The stress response of the FE-RVE is given by 〈   σ  i j 〉 =   1  V    ∫  Ω       σ  i j ⅆ  V ≃     Σ  k = 1 , n   σ  i j  k   V  k    Σ  k = 1 , n   V  k . This stress response can then be used to validate the prediction given by the mean-field homogenization. The FE-RVE can also be used to obtain the stiffness matrix by performing a linear perturbation step with six load cases, with each load case yielding one column of the 6 × 6 matrix. The Coefficient of Thermal Expansions (CTE) can be computed by applying a uniform temperature change across the FE-RVE and measuring the change in the volume-averaged strain. Results and discussion A two-step mean-field homogenization method is used to obtain the transverse response of Composite 1. Both Mori-Tanaka and the balanced homogenization model are used in Step 1, and Voigt homogenization is used in Step 2. Results are compared with the FE-RVE prediction in [Table 1](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-linearelastic-table__simaver-c-multiscale-twostep-table). The mean-field predictions show good agreement with the FE-RVE results.  The influence of the volume fraction on the normalized macroscale modulus of Composite 2 is plotted in [Figure 2](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-linearelastic-figs__simaver-c-multiscale-figure-sphere-vf) using various homogenization methods. Two different FE-RVEs are used for comparison: body-centered and square packed. The body-centered FE-RVE is shown in [Figure 1](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-linearelastic-figs__simaver-c-multscale-sphere-center). The square-packed FE-RVE is also a cubic cell with only a single sphere in the middle of the cell. The Mori-Tanaka prediction matches the body-centered FE-RVE prediction very well, while the balanced prediction matches the square-packed FE-RVE results very well up to a volume fraction of 50%. Interaction between inclusions is stronger in the squared-packed FE-RVE compared to the body-centered FE-RVE, which can be better captured with the balanced method.  Composites with various off-axis angles are tested with Composite 3. The influence of the off-axis angle on the normalized macroscale modulus in the traction direction is plotted in [Figure 3](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-linearelastic-figs__simaver-c-multiscale-figure-angle). Mori-Tanaka and the balanced homogenization methods are used, and the predicted macroscale properties compare well with the FE-RVE results. Results and discussion Nonlinear macroscale properties predicted by mean-field homogenization are validated.  The macroscale response of Composite 1 under uniaxial tension and simple shear loading is plotted in [Figure 16](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-nonlinear-figures__simaver-c-multiscale-sphere-plastic) and [Figure 17](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-nonlinear-figures__simaver-c-multiscale-nonlinear-simpleshear-sphere). The Mori-Tanaka homogenization method is used. The results compare well with the FE-RVE prediction. The results from both the uniaxial tension and the simple shear tests show that the isotropization methods have little influence on the prediction for this type of composite.  The macroscale response of Composite 2 under uniaxial tension loading is plotted in [Figure 18](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-nonlinear-figures__simaver-c-multiscale-prolate-plastic-random3d). The Mori-Tanaka method is used for homogenization. 10 × 10 angle subdivisions are used to compute the numerical integration over orientation angles. The results are compared with those presented by [Doghri (2006)](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-nonlinear-refs__simaver-c-multiscale-doghri2006).  The macroscale response of Composite 3 under uniaxial tension loading is plotted in [Figure 19](simaver-c-multiscale.md#simaver-c-multiscale-nonlinear-figures__simaver-c-multiscale-visplas-sphere). The Mori-Tanaka homogenization method is used. The results compare well with the FE-RVE prediction. Composite cure modeling Elements tested * C3D8RT * CAX4RT * CPE4RT * CPE4RHT  * DC2D4 * DC3D8 * DCAX4  Features tested Multiscale materials with cure modeling.  Problem description These tests verify the multiscale material model when cure properties are specified in the matrix material. Material properties specified in [Cure modeling in Abaqus/Standard](simaver-c-curemodel.md) are used in these tests for the matrix material. The fiber volume fraction is set to a very small value; therefore, the results are identical to the test results in [Cure modeling in Abaqus/Standard](simaver-c-curemodel.md).  Results and discussion The results of the multiscale model tests are identical with the results of the cure modeling tests in [Implementation using dedicated cure modeling options](simaver-c-curemodel.md#simaver-c-curemodel-inputfiles__simaver-c-curemodel-dedicated) and [Bilayer test](simaver-c-curemodel.md#simaver-c-curemodel-inputfiles__simaver-c-curemodel-bilayer).
