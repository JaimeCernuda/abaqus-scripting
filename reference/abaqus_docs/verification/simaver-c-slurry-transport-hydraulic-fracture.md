Both XFEM and cohesive elements are used to model the fracture with slurry transport and placement in this analysis. Two different models verify different aspects of the slurry transport and placement:   * Slot test to verify slurry advection and settling in fixed-opening   fractures * Hydraulic fracture test to verify the crack profile and slurry advection   In the slot test, the dimensions of the slot channel are 1 m long, 0.2 m high, and 0.5 mm wide. The injection rate is 0.0004 m3/h. The injected proppant has the following properties: C  V  i n l e t = 0.2  ρ  w = 1000 ⁢   k g /   m  3 ,   ρ  s = 1250 ⁢   k g /   m  3 The diameter of the proppant particles is assumed to be      D  p = 0.01 ⁢     m m .  In the hydraulic fracture test, a small initial crack is specified at one end of the specimen. A two-step analysis is performed to simulate the hydraulic fracture propagation. First, a geostatic step is performed to establish the initial equilibrium state. The next step represents the hydraulic fracture stage, where the slurry with a proppant concentration of 0.6 is injected into the well with an injection rate of 50 m3/h. Similar studies are conducted through applied pore pressure of 120 MPa. The element adjacent to the wellbore is defined as initially open to permit entry of the slurry. The duration of this stage is 0.1 s, and during this time period both the applied volumetric flow rate (or the applied pore pressure) and concentration are ramped up linearly. Additional tests using the same model exercise the following:  * leakoff with a dynamic filtration model; * user subroutine to set crack opening offset for contact of crack surfaces; * minimum ratio of the crack opening to the particle size, necessary to activate   the slurry calculations; and * deactivate the slurry flow in an analysis.  Results and discussion The results for the XFEM and cohesive approaches are compared to each other, as well as with available analytical solutions obtained by solving a one-dimensional slurry flow continuity equation (accounting for leakoff). Good agreement is obtained in all cases.
