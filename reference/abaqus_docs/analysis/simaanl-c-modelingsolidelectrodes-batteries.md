The anode and cathode regions are separated by an electrically insulating porous separator, which allows ions to move across from anode to cathode and vice versa. [Figure 1](#simaanl-c-modelingsolidelectrodes-batteries-overview__lithium-battery-schem) shows a typical configuration of a solid lithium battery. Lithium metal batteries tend to have a higher capacity and reduced size compared to batteries with all porous electrodes. Figure 1. Schematic diagram of a solid lithium metal battery.     Since the anode is not porous, the Newman porous electrode theory framework is not required to model the anode. Instead, the behavior of the anode can be characterized using classical thermal-electrical-structural physics. The separator and cathode regions are modeled as porous electrodes as described in [Coupled Thermal-Electrochemical Analysis](simaanl-c-coupthermalelecchem.md). The Abaqus lithium metal battery models assume that the interface between the separator and the anode can be characterized using a surface-based Butler-Volmer load (see [Figure 1](#simaanl-c-modelingsolidelectrodes-batteries-overview__lithium-battery-schem)) that accounts for fluid electric potential and ion concentration on the separator side and the solid electric potential on the anode side.  Governing Equations The governing equations for the thermal-electrochemical process are based on the porous electrode theory and are described in detail in [Governing Equations](simaanl-c-coupthermalelecchem.md#simaanl-c-coupthermalelecchem-governingeq) in [Coupled Thermal-Electrochemical Analysis](simaanl-c-coupthermalelecchem.md). At the interface between the separator and the solid lithium anode, a charge-transfer reaction occurs following a Butler-Volmer kinetics that is expressed using the following equations: Li - Θ ↔   Li  + +   e  - + Θ . Here,   Li - Θ  represents lithium in the solid electrode that, through the deintercalation reaction, transforms into a lithium ion in the electrolyte,    Li + , plus an electron,    e - , and leaves behind a vacancy (or intercalation site) in the solid,   Θ . Abaqus/Standard offers two options to define the Butler-Volmer surface interaction.  When the two regions (namely, the solid lithium anode and the porous separator) are modeled using matching meshes with shared nodes, you can define the Butler-Volmer interaction as a surface-based load on the facets of the anode elements.  If the meshes on the two sides of the interface between the solid anode and the separator are dissimilar and do not share nodes, you can use a surface interaction property to define an interface reaction behavior.  For both definitions you must specify the name of a table collection that includes the corresponding Butler-Volmer kinetics properties for the interface ([Defining Properties for Butler-Volmer Kinetics](#simaanl-c-modelingsolidelectrodes-batteries-BVproperties)).
