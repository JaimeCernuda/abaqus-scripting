The material has two sets of planes of weakness having an included angle of 2α. We seek to construct the failure envelope of the material as the orientation (θ in [Figure 1](simabmk-c-jointedmat.md#simabmk-c-jointedmat-t-exafiguresect1__simabmk-c-sxmuniaxial-geom)) of the planes of weakness is varied.  In the Abaqus model the failure surface for sliding on the joint systems is defined as  fa=τa-pa⁢tan⁡βa-da=0,  where pa is the pressure stress across the joint, τa is the shear stress magnitude in the joint, βa is the friction angle of the joint, and da is its cohesion. For this problem we assume that for both joints da=​1000 (the units are not important), βa=​ 45°, and that plastic flow in the joints is associated.  The behavior of the bulk material is based on the Drucker-Prager failure criterion  q-p⁢tan⁡βb-db=0,  where q⁢=def⁢32⁢SS is the Mises equivalent deviatoric stress (here S is the deviatoric stress S⁢=def⁢σ+p⁢I), p⁢=def-13⁢I:σ is the equivalent pressure stress, βb is the friction angle of the bulk material, and db is the cohesion of the bulk material. For this problem we assume that db=​ 8000, βb=​ 45°, and that plastic flow of the bulk material is associated.  When all the joints are closed, the material is assumed to be isotropic and linear elastic with a Young's modulus of 3 × 105 and a Poisson's ratio of 0.3. When a joint opens, the material is assumed to have no elastic stiffness with respect to direct strain across the joint system or with respect to shearing associated with this direction. Open joints, thus, create anisotropic elastic response.  Each test performed in this example is carried out using a cube (one C3D8 element) of unit dimensions. Displacements are prescribed at the nodes of the cube to simulate homogeneous deformation and stress conditions.  Results and discussion [Figure 2](simabmk-c-jointedmat.md#simabmk-c-jointedmat-t-exafiguresect1__simabmk-c-sxmuniaxial-failure) shows the variation of the compressive failure stress P/2⁢da with θ, the angle which the bisector of the joints forms with the direction of the load. Compression failure envelopes are developed for α=​ 0°, 20°, 30°. It is clear that for certain ranges of orientation of the joints with respect to the loading direction, failure along the planes of weakness becomes increasingly improbable and failure of the bulk material takes place first. It may be noted that the case when α=​0° corresponds to the theory of a single plane of weakness proposed by Jeager (1960).  When the load is applied in tension, the material cannot carry any stress since the joints open readily.
